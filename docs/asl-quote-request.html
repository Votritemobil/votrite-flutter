VotRite — ASL Video Translation: Scope & Quote Request

Project: VotRite accessible online voting (votritemobil.com) What we need: Pre-recorded American Sign Language (ASL) video clips that play inside the voting app to guide Deaf and hard-of-hearing voters through the ballot. The app already has the ASL player built in — we drop your finished MP4 files in and they appear automatically. No production work on our side.

1. The two kinds of clips (this drives your quote)

Our content splits into two buckets. Please price them separately.

Bucket A — Fixed interface clips (recorded ONCE, rarely change)

These 16 clips cover the app’s screens and buttons. The English source text for each is in Section 3. They are one-time work.

Bucket B — Ballot-content clips (RE-RECORDED every election)

Each election the candidates and ballot measures change, so these must be re-recorded each time: - One clip per candidate (their name + party + office) - One clip per ballot measure / proposition (the full measure text)

Bucket B is a recurring cost for the life of the system, so the per-clip re-record price and turnaround are the most important numbers for us. A single election typically has roughly 15–30 candidate clips and 3–8 measure clips, but please quote per-clip so it scales to any ballot.

2. What we’re asking you to quote

Please break your quote out as: 1. Price per fixed clip (Bucket A) and total for all 16. 2. Price per candidate clip (Bucket B, short). 3. Price per ballot-measure clip (Bucket B, longer — measure text can be several sentences). 4. Turnaround for the initial full set, and turnaround for per-clip re-records when ballot content changes ahead of an election. 5. Any rush option and cost. 6. Whether re-records reuse the same interpreter, wardrobe, and background for visual consistency across elections.

3. Fixed interface clips — English source text (Bucket A)

  1. Welcome — “Welcome to VotRite online voting. This system lets you vote privately and securely. To begin, choose Start.”
  2. How to Vote — “Here is how voting works. You will see one contest at a time. For each contest, choose the candidate or candidates you want, then go to the next page. At the end you can review all your choices before you cast your ballot.”
  3. Choose Your Ballot — “Choose your ballot. Select the election you are voting in from the list.”
  4. Choose a Language — “Choose your language. Select the language you would like to use to vote.”
  5. Enter Your PIN — “Enter your PIN code. Type the secret code you were given to begin voting.”
  6. Vote for a Candidate — “This is a contest. Choose the candidate you want. You may select up to the number shown on the screen. Choose a name to select it, and choose it again to remove it.”
  7. Candidate (generic) — “This is a candidate. Their name and party are shown on the screen.” (Specific candidates are Bucket B.)
  8. Ballot Measure (generic) — “This is a ballot measure. Read the measure, then vote Yes or No.” (Specific measures are Bucket B.)
  9. Vote YES — “Vote Yes. Choose Yes if you approve this measure.”
  10. Vote NO — “Vote No. Choose No if you do not approve this measure.”
  11. Next Page — “Next. Go on to the next page.”
  12. Go Back — “Back. Return to the previous page.”
  13. Review Your Choices — “Review your choices. Check all of your selections. If something is not right, you can go back and change it.”
  14. Cast Your Ballot — “Cast your ballot. When you are ready, cast your ballot. After you cast it, you cannot make changes.”
  15. Voting Help — “Help. If you need assistance, ask a poll worker or use the on-screen help.”
  16. Your Vote Was Cast — “Your vote has been cast. Thank you for voting.”

4. Ballot-content clips — templates (Bucket B)

We will send a per-election sheet listing each item. Each clip’s source is:

Candidate clip — the candidate’s full name (to be fingerspelled), party, and office. Example source: > “Jane Smith. Democratic Party. Candidate for Mayor.”

Ballot-measure clip — the measure’s title followed by its full text, verbatim, translated faithfully. Example source: > “Measure A. Shall the city issue twelve million dollars in bonds to modernize public schools, with citizen oversight and independent audits?”

For each election we provide a spreadsheet (item ID, type, and exact English source text) so each clip maps to the right place in the app automatically.

5. Technical delivery specs

  • Format: MP4, H.264 video, yuv420p, web-optimized (+faststart).
  • Aspect ratio: 4:3 (the in-app player panel is 4:3). Recommended resolution 960×720 (640×480 minimum).
  • Frame rate: 30 fps.
  • Audio: not required — the app shows its own text caption under the video. A silent track is fine; an English voice track is optional.
  • Framing / look: interpreter centered, framed head to mid-torso with full signing space visible; solid high-contrast background (mid-blue or neutral gray); dark, solid, non-patterned clothing; even lighting.
  • No burned-in captions, names, or logos — the app overlays its own caption text.
  • Delivery: individual .mp4 files, one per clip. For fixed clips we’ll give you the exact filenames; for ballot content, name each file by the item ID on the sheet.

6. Quality & credentials required

  • Nationally certified interpreter(s) — RID/NAD.
  • A Certified Deaf Interpreter (CDI) on the filmed talent for ballot content.
  • Independent second-interpreter QA review on every delivery.
  • Written accuracy sign-off with each delivery (required — this is an election).
